    01
    Simple House Wiring
    Single-phase radial circuit
    Beginner
    A basic residential circuit where 230V AC passes through an MCB for overcurrent protection, then a switch to control a lamp. The neutral returns directly to supply. An earth conductor provides safety fault-current path.
    1
    Supply Entry: 230V AC enters the distribution board. Live (L) is red/brown; Neutral (N) is black/blue.
    2
    MCB Protection: The MCB monitors current. If it exceeds its rating (e.g. 16A) it trips, isolating the circuit automatically.
    3
    Switch Control: The switch is always in the Live conductor — never Neutral — so the load is fully de-energised when switched off.
    4
    Load (Lamp): Current flows through the lamp, completing the circuit back through the Neutral to the supply.
    5
    Earth: The Earth wire connects all metallic enclosures. A live-to-metal fault sends current to earth, tripping the MCB or RCD.

    02
    DOL Starter Circuit
    Direct On Line motor starter
    Intermediate
    A DOL starter applies full 3-phase voltage directly to an induction motor. It uses a main power contactor, thermal overload relay, and a latching control circuit with START/STOP pushbuttons.
    1
    Start: Pressing START energises the KM coil. Main power contacts close, connecting full 3-phase supply to the motor.
    2
    Seal-in: An auxiliary NO contact of KM wires in parallel with START, keeping the coil energised after the button is released.
    3
    Stop: Pressing STOP breaks the control circuit. The coil de-energises, power contacts open, disconnecting the motor.
    4
    Overload: The thermal OL relay monitors motor current. If it exceeds the FLC setting, the OL trips its NC contact, cutting the coil.
    5
    Suitability: DOL is suitable for motors up to ~7.5 kW. Larger motors use star-delta or soft-starters to limit high starting currents (6–8× FLC).

    03
    Two-Way Switch Wiring
    Staircase / hotel switch circuit
    Intermediate
    Two-way switching uses two SPDT switches to control one light from two locations — the classic staircase circuit. Three wires run between them: one Common and two Traveller conductors.
    1
    Switch Anatomy: Each SPDT switch has three terminals — Common (C), L1, and L2. The common connects to either L1 or L2 depending on switch position.
    2
    Traveller Wires: Two wires (T1 and T2) run between the L1 and L2 terminals of both switches, carrying current between them.
    3
    Light ON: The circuit is complete when both switches connect to the same traveller — both on T1, or both on T2.
    4
    Light OFF: When switches are on opposite travellers — one on T1, the other on T2 — the circuit is broken and the lamp is off.
    5
    Fault Diagnosis: If the light won't switch from one location, check the traveller wires for continuity. If neither location works, check the Common wire and the lamp.
